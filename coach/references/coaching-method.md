# Coaching method

Practical, evidence-based, built for recreational runners with jobs and kids. The best
plan is the one they'll actually do. Consistency beats heroics, every time.

## Core principles

1. **Mostly easy.** Roughly 80% of running time easy (Z1-Z2, can hold a conversation),
   ~20% moderate-to-hard. Most recreational runners run their easy days too fast; telling
   them to slow down is often the single most useful thing you'll say.
2. **Progress gently.** Build weekly volume gradually; as a rule of thumb no more than
   ~10% week on week once established, and hold or cut back every 3rd-4th week. Beginners
   and returners progress time on feet before pace.
3. **One thing at a time.** Don't raise volume and intensity in the same week.
4. **Hard days hard, easy days easy.** Never two quality sessions back to back. A long run
   counts as a hard-ish day.
5. **Recovery is training.** Sleep, stress and life load show up in pace and heart rate.
   A bad week at work is a reason to ease off, not push through.
6. **Specificity near the goal.** Far out: build aerobic base. Closer in: practise goal pace.
7. **Life wins.** When the plan and life collide, change the plan without fuss.

## Session catalogue

Use these names. Fit paces to the runner's own data.

| Session | What it is | Purpose | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Easy run | 20-60 min conversational | Aerobic base, recovery | The bulk of the week |
| Long run | The longest run of the week, easy | Endurance, durability | ~25-30% of weekly volume, cap growth carefully |
| Strides | 4-8 × 15-20 s quick, relaxed, full recovery | Form, leg speed | Add to the end of an easy run, nearly free |
| Tempo / threshold | 15-30 min "comfortably hard", or 3-4 × 8-10 min | Lactate threshold | Can speak a few words, not sentences |
| Norwegian 4x4 | 4 × 4 min hard (Z4-Z5) with 3 min easy jog | VO2 max | Judge by work pace and rep spread, never whole-run average |
| Intervals | e.g. 6-8 × 400-800 m, or 5 × 3 min | VO2 max, speed | |
| Hills | 6-10 × 30-90 s uphill hard, jog down | Strength, power | Good substitute for speed work |
| Progression run | Easy, finishing the last 10-20% at steady/tempo | Pacing, finishing strength | |
| parkrun / time trial | 5 km all-out | Fitness check, fun | Counts as the week's quality session |
| Cooper test | 12 min max distance | VO2 max estimate | A test, not a race; keep its name |
| Recovery run | Very easy, short | Blood flow | Optional; rest is fine too |

## Pace guidance from their data

- Easy: roughly 75-110 s/km slower than their recent 5 km pace (e.g. 5 km at 4:39/km →
  easy ~5:55-6:30/km), or simply Z1-Z2 HR. Give the range, not the fast end, and never a
  range that starts at a pace you just called too fast.
- Tempo: roughly 20-30 s/km slower than 5 km pace.
- 4x4 / VO2: around 5 km pace or a touch faster for 4 min reps.
- Long run: easy pace, slower is fine.
- If HR zones and pace disagree, trust effort and HR on hot, hilly or tired days.

Always say it's an estimate when you derive pace from one race or test.

## Common situations

- **Missed one session:** don't cram it in. Skip it or slot it where spacing allows.
- **Missed a week:** resume at the last week's easier version, not where they'd be.
- **Back after 2+ weeks off:** start around 50-70% of previous volume, easy only for the
  first week, rebuild over 2-3 weeks.
- **Mild cold, above the neck:** easy and short is OK if they feel fine; or rest.
- **Fever, chest symptoms, body aches, a stomach bug, covid or flu:** no running while sick.
  Return only once they've been fever-free for a full day without fever-reducing medication
  and feel normal. Then easy runs only, for at least as many days as they were sick; quality
  only after that, and once easy runs feel normal. Say what happens to each planned session
  this week (quality dropped, long run dropped or cut, easy runs left or cut), and use numbers
  from the plan and data only. Why, in one line if asked: running with a viral illness carries
  a heart (myocarditis) risk. Any chest pain, palpitations or unusual breathlessness on return
  = stop and see a doctor.
- **Heat:** slow down 10-30 s/km or run by effort; hydrate; avoid midday.
- **Hills / trail:** compare by effort or by the same trail, never against flat road pace.
- **Race week (taper):** cut volume ~30-50% over the last 7-10 days for a 10 km+; keep a
  little intensity; nothing new on race day.
- **Two runs in one day:** fine for experienced runners if both are easy-ish; not for beginners.
- **Running with a niggle:** see `safety.md`. Pain that changes how they run = stop.

## Realistic goals

Use their data to check a goal. Rough rules:
- Race time prediction: Riegel (T2 = T1 × (D2/D1)^1.06) from the race **closest in distance**
  (a 10 km beats a 5 km for a half). Riegel from short races runs fast for runners on modest
  volume or whose long run is still short of ~16-18 km, so give a range, lean to the slower
  end, and say it assumes the long runs get done. Check both PBs and say which you used.
- A goal check names a concrete realistic number and a concrete stretch number, not "could
  push it a bit".
- A 5 km improvement of ~1-3% per training block is realistic for an established runner;
  beginners improve faster.
- First half marathon: they want to be comfortably running ~15+ km weekly and a ~8-10 km
  long run before starting a 10-12 week build.

If a goal looks unrealistic, say so kindly and offer the realistic version and the
stretch version.
