Run Supreme privacy policy

Applies to the Run Supreme Android app. Last updated 25 September 2026.

Run Supreme is a running app with no account, no ads and no analytics of our own. This page says plainly what stays on your phone, what two outside services receive so the app can work, and what you can turn off.

1. Your runs stay on your phone

Everything Run Supreme records is stored on your device: your recorded route (GPS points), lap and rep times, pace, heart rate from a paired strap, the verdicts and trends the app computes from them, your settings, and any plan you follow. We do not run a server, we do not have an account for you, and we never see any of this.

Android's automatic backup may copy your run files to your Google account as part of your device backup, under Google's backup terms and your device settings. You can turn device backup off in Android settings. Files you export (JSON or TCX) go wherever you choose to send them.

2. Google Maps

After a run, Run Supreme draws your route on a Google map. The map is provided by the Google Maps SDK for Android, which runs inside the app and talks to Google directly to fetch map tiles. According to Google's own disclosure for this SDK, Google receives:

  • your IP address (which gives an approximate location),
  • a Maps SDK identifier that Google uses to count daily active users of the SDK,
  • request metadata and crash data from the SDK,
  • map interaction and camera events, which describe the map area you are viewing. Because the post-run map is zoomed in on your route, that map area amounts to a precise location.

In short: Google sees the map area you view and your IP address, like any maps app. Google does not receive your run file, your lap times or your heart rate from us. Google's handling of what it receives is covered by the Google Privacy Policy. The map only loads when you open a run's detail screen; there is no map while you are recording.

3. Weather from Open-Meteo

To adjust for heat and humidity, Run Supreme can fetch the weather for each run from Open-Meteo. The request contains your run's location rounded to about 10 km, and the run's date and time. It contains no identifier: no account, no device ID, no name. Open-Meteo's servers see the IP address the request comes from, as any web request does. Weather data is provided by Open-Meteo.com under the CC BY 4.0 licence.

You can turn this off in Settings, under Recording, with the "Weather for each run" switch. With it off, nothing is sent to Open-Meteo.

5. What we do not do

  • No accounts, sign-in or profiles.
  • No advertising, no ad SDKs.
  • No analytics or tracking of our own. The only analytics-type signal is the Maps SDK identifier described in section 2, which belongs to Google's SDK, not to us.
  • We do not sell or share your data. We could not: we never hold it.
  • Run Supreme is not directed at children and has no features aimed at them.

6. Your controls

  • Delete everything: Settings, Data, "Delete all" removes every run and setting from the phone. Uninstalling the app does the same. What Google's Maps SDK already received (section 2) is held by Google, not us; your Google account's privacy controls apply to it.
  • Weather: Settings, Recording, "Weather for each run".
  • Map: the map appears only on a run's detail screen; do not open it if you would rather Google did not receive that map area.
  • Backup: Android settings, Google, Backup.
  • Export: Settings, Data, "Export all" gives you your data as JSON or TCX.
