Run Supreme privacy policy

Applies to the Run Supreme Android app. Last updated 17 October 2026.

Run Supreme is a running app with no account and no ads. The companion (section 2b) counts button taps and activity per tester; see "Counting taps and activity". This page says plainly what stays on your phone, what outside services receive so the app can work, and what you can turn off. There is one optional feature, the Run companion (section 2b), that sends run summaries to our server. It is off unless you turn it on.

1. Your runs stay on your phone unless you choose to send them

Everything Run Supreme records is stored on your device: your recorded route (GPS points), lap and rep times, pace, heart rate from a paired strap, the verdicts and trends the app computes from them, your settings, and any plan you follow. We do not have an account for you, and we never see any of this. The one exception is the optional Run companion (sections 2b and 2c): if you turn it on, a short summary of each run is sent to our server.

Android's automatic backup may copy your run files to your Google account as part of your device backup, under Google's backup terms and your device settings. You can turn device backup off in Android settings. Files you export (JSON, TCX or GPX) go wherever you choose to send them.

Health Connect (Android's health data store) is one of those services. It is off by default. If you switch it on, or tap Send to Health Connect on a run, Run Supreme writes that run to Health Connect on your phone: the exercise session with its laps and pauses, the heart rate, the distance and, if you allow it, the route (without the first and last 200 m). Sending the same run again replaces it, never duplicates it. From there, the apps you allow in Health Connect can read it under their own policies. Run Supreme does not read anything from Health Connect.

Run detail has a Send button. It makes a TCX or GPX file of that run (the first and last 200 m of the route are left out) and opens Android's share sheet, so you pick the app that receives it. The Strava row only opens Strava's upload page in your browser; you upload the file yourself. Start's session card and a saved route have Send to watch: it makes a workout file (.fit) of the session, or a GPX course of the route, and opens the same share sheet. The course holds the whole route, including where it starts and ends, because a watch needs the full line to navigate it. Settings has a Send runs to section for services that can receive runs automatically. Every one is off by default, and a run goes to a service only after you switch it on. Once a file is with another app or service, that app's own privacy policy applies.

A run, your scores and a moment (such as a first or a personal best) can be made into a share card: a picture made on your phone that opens the same share sheet when you tap Share. A run card shows its title, distance, time, pace and the day it was run, and the shape of the route only if you switch on Show map. The Your pack section of Progress can share your pack result: just your age band and sex group (for example men 40-49) and a line such as faster than 6 in 10, worked out on your phone from published norms. No name, place, date, route or other runner is on a pack card, and nothing is uploaded until you pick an app in the share sheet.

Intervals.icu (only if you connect it)

Intervals.icu receives data from Run Supreme only after you connect your own account: in Settings, Send runs to, you paste your personal API key and athlete id (both come from Intervals.icu, Settings, Developer settings). Nothing is sent to Intervals.icu before that, and nothing is sent for runs recorded before you switched it on. When it is connected, Run Supreme uploads a run's TCX file (route, lap times and heart rate, with the first and last 200 m of the route left out), the run's title, and the run's id, which Intervals.icu uses to refuse a second copy of the same run. You can also send a single run from its Send button. The key is stored in your phone's Android Keystore-backed secure storage, is sent only to Intervals.icu, is never written to logs, and is not part of Android's device backup. Settings, Send runs to, Intervals.icu, Manage connection, Disconnect deletes the key from the phone. Runs already uploaded stay in your Intervals.icu account, where Intervals.icu's own privacy policy applies; you can delete them there.

If Intervals.icu is connected, Send to watch can also add a planned workout to your Intervals.icu calendar, only when you tap Add for today or Add for tomorrow. That sends the session's name and steps (lengths, any heart rate or pace range, repeats), the date you chose, and an id that lets a second send replace the first. It contains no run, route or heart rate history. Intervals.icu then passes it on to the watch service you linked there (for example Garmin, COROS or Suunto), under their policies. Delete it from your Intervals.icu calendar to remove it.

Routes you follow

Start has a "Follow a route" choice for Free, Trail and Goal runs. You can pick a GPX or TCX file with Android's file picker, or turn one of your own past runs into a route. Run Supreme reads only the file you pick, on your phone, and keeps a simplified copy of the route (its points and, if the file has them, elevations) in the app's own storage. The file itself is not copied or sent anywhere, and nothing about the route leaves the phone: the distance left, the "off route" alert and the turn hints (worked out from the route's shape, not from any map or trail data) are all calculated on the phone, and the alert and hints are spoken by your phone's own voice. When you run a route, a copy of it, and how long you spent off it, is saved with that run's file, so run detail can show the route you meant against the one you ran; it goes wherever the run goes if you export it as JSON (a TCX or GPX Send carries only your own track). Saved routes are part of the app's stored data, so they are included in Android's automatic backup with your runs, and you can delete any of them from Start, Follow a route.

2. Google Maps

After a run, and during a run if you switch the record screen to MAP, Run Supreme draws your route on a Google map (for Free and Trail runs, Google's terrain map with its contour lines; a route you are following is drawn on top by Run Supreme, and is not sent to Google). The map is provided by the Google Maps SDK for Android, which runs inside the app and talks to Google directly to fetch map tiles. According to Google's own disclosure for this SDK, Google receives:

  • your IP address (which gives an approximate location),
  • a Maps SDK identifier that Google uses to count daily active users of the SDK,
  • request metadata and crash data from the SDK,
  • map interaction and camera events, which describe the map area you are viewing. Because the post-run map is zoomed in on your route, that map area amounts to a precise location. The live map during a run follows you, so while MAP is showing, the area Google receives is where you are at that moment.

In short: Google sees the map area you view and your IP address, like any maps app. Google does not receive your run file, your lap times or your heart rate from us. Google's handling of what it receives is covered by the Google Privacy Policy. The map loads when you open a run's detail screen, map images for your recent runs load from Google when Home shows them and are kept on your phone, and the map loads while MAP is selected on the record screen during a run. NUMBERS is the default, and no map loads while it is showing or while the screen is off. Run Supreme does not send your position to Google; Google only sees the map area the map asks for.

Offline maps

Offline maps you choose to download come from our map storage (Cloudflare); the download reveals which region you picked and your IP address, nothing else. Maps then work offline. Nothing else is sent with the download: no account, no device ID, no run data. Cloudflare sees the IP address the request comes from, as any web request does. Maps only download when you tap Download in Settings, under Offline maps.

2a. Offline maps (Topo)

For Free and Trail runs, Run Supreme can draw the route on its own offline Topo map instead of Google's map, where you have downloaded the map for that area (Settings, Offline maps) and Topo is the map you picked on the run screen or run detail. Topo is drawn on your phone from a map file you downloaded once. Drawing it makes no network request at all: no tiles are fetched, no Google service is used, and your position and your route never leave the phone. Where no downloaded map covers the run, or you pick Google, the Google map in section 2 is used.

The only request is the download itself, and only when you tap Download (or Refresh) in Settings, Offline maps. It goes to Run Supreme's own map hosting (Cloudflare R2) and carries the file you asked for, that is the region's id such as "milos" in the address, and your IP address, like any download. It contains no account, device ID, location, run or route. The hosting is not told which runs you have or where you run; it can see which regions are downloaded. Delete a region in Settings, Offline maps to remove its file from your phone.

Map data: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), basemap tiles by Protomaps, terrain by Mapterhorn, with Copernicus DEM data. The map shows this credit on screen and Settings, About lists it in full.

2b. Run companion (optional)

The Run companion sends a short note to your Telegram after a run, with the numbers that matter. It may ask how a run felt, suggest up to two sessions a week, and check in with you before and after a planned run (see "Check-ins" below). WhatsApp is planned for later. It is off unless you turn it on. The setup screen and Settings, Companion have two equal buttons, Turn on and Not now, and neither is pre-selected. Until you turn it on, nothing in this section applies and nothing is sent.

What is sent, and when

After each run, once the companion is on, the app sends a summary of that run:

  • the run type, date and start time, and your time zone;
  • duration, distance, pace and True Pace;
  • the verdict, minutes in each heart rate zone, and climb;
  • for an interval session: the session's name, how many reps you did, your work pace (hills and heat taken out, the number the verdict shows), the gap between your fastest and slowest rep, and your recovery pace. The name of one of the app's built-in sessions, such as Norwegian 4x4, goes as is. If you built the session yourself, its name is never sent: only a short code made from it (the words themselves never leave your phone), so the companion can tell it is the same session again. The writing service (OpenRouter) is only told the name of a built-in session;
  • the name of the area where you started (the suburb, never the street);
  • how the run felt (Easy, Right or Hard), if you tapped it;
  • your pace for each kilometre, with the heart rate zone you mostly ran it in, and, for a structured session such as intervals, the time and zone of each rep;
  • unless you turn off "Show route shape on cards" (Settings, Companion, on by default): an outline of your route with the first and last stretch cut off. See "The route outline" below.
  • only for a run the app has matched to a trail you have run before: your own times on that trail (a list of up to 10 whole-second times with the attempt number of each, so "Run 4, 1:01:23"; no dates), how many times you have run it, a rough elevation profile of the trail (with the position of its turning point, and whether your phone measured it with its barometer or with GPS), how far ahead of or behind your best you were along the way, and whether it is an out and back, a loop or one way, and the trail's name only if you turned on the place option. See "Trail pictures" below. Only your own runs: no other runner is ever compared with you.
  • only if the app has offered you a quest: the quest's kind (for example runs of a trail, or distance in 30 days), its target and your own best to compare it with, as whole numbers, and, once you take one, how far along you are and the date it ends, and how it ended (finished, dropped or lapsed) or that you chose Not this month. The trail's name goes only if you turned on the place option. The app sends no run ids with a quest, no coordinates and no dates other than the end date, the month it was offered and when you took it. See "Quests" below;
  • only if you turn on "Heart rate on cards" (Settings, Companion, off until you turn it on), and only for a trail run recorded with a heart rate strap: your heart rate along the run as at most 64 whole bpm values, each with your own zone (1 to 5). See "Heart rate on trail pictures" below;
  • only if you follow a 4-week plan in the app: which planned session the run was (easy, long or quality), which week of the plan it fell in (for example week 3 of 4), the distance the plan asked for when it names one, and the next planned session: its type, its planned day (a date), its week, its minutes or distance when the plan has them. When that session is one of the app's built-in sessions (for example "Norwegian 4x4" or "Tempo intervals"), its name goes too, together with its built-in code (for example norwegian_4x4). The server accepts a session name only if it is exactly one of the built-in names. For a quality session the work pace the app targets for it also goes (your True Pace, hills and heat taken out, as seconds per km), with whether it came from your fitness or from a pace you moved yourself; an easy or long session sends no pace. Only built-in session names are sent: a session you built yourself is never named, and nothing you typed is ever sent. This is what the plan already holds on your phone. No reasons and no race date go with it, and the app has no race date to send;
  • only if you turn on "Weather in suggestions" (Settings, Companion, off until you turn it on): the area where the run started, rounded on your phone to about 11 km (0.1 degree), with no place name.

The plan details let a note say things like "your plan has a long run tomorrow, week 3 of 4", and let the evening suggestion follow the session your plan already has for the next day. If you have no plan in the app, none of this is sent and suggestions work as before. They are in the data the server returns about you (the data export); the app's "What the companion knows" screen does not show them. Delete everything removes them with the run they came from.

It never sends your GPS points, your position, a map, the street, or your heart rate trace (the one exception is the separate, off-by-default "Heart rate on cards" switch, see "Heart rate on trail pictures" below). Runs recorded before you turned the companion on are not sent. If the phone is offline the summary is queued and sent later.

The Mini App (optional)

If the Run companion's Mini App is switched on, a button in the chat opens a page inside Telegram that shows your week, your latest run's reps or splits, and your four lanes. It shows only what the companion already holds from the summaries above, and it collects nothing new. It does not show the pack or your age band, your route outline, or the area name. Telegram sees that you opened the Mini App, and the page it displays, because it runs inside Telegram. To know it is you, Telegram gives the page a signed token that our server checks; the token expires after an hour and we do not store or log it.

Trail pictures

When you run a trail you have run before, the coach can send two kinds of picture to your own Telegram chat: a trail board (your own runs of that trail, ranked, from your third run) and a trail record (when you set a new best on it). What is sent for them is only whole numbers: your pace-normalised times, the elevation profile (at most 64 heights in whole metres, counted up or down from where the trail starts, so there is no absolute altitude and no coordinates) and the seconds you were ahead or behind your best along the way. Your phone works out which of your runs are the same trail and which times to send: nothing about it is matched on our server. The trail's name is a place, so it is sent and drawn only if you have turned on the place option for sharing; otherwise the picture says "Your trail". Place names are off by default, are kept only while that option is on, and are deleted from our server at once when you turn it off. The elevation profile is not an outline of your route and does not depend on "Show route shape on cards", because it only appears in your own chat. If you forward such a picture, anyone you send it to can see what it shows, which is why the app only draws the profile on a picture you share from the app when "Show route shape on cards" is on. Trail pictures arrive hidden under a spoiler that you tap to reveal. The profile is a relative shape, not a place: whole metres up or down from the start, at most 64 of them, no latitude or longitude, no altitude above sea level. All of it is kept for 30 days after its run, then deleted, is in the data the server returns about you, and is deleted at once when you delete your data.

Heart rate on trail pictures

Heart rate is health data, so it is off until you turn on "Heart rate on cards" in Settings, Companion. With it on, and only for a trail run you recorded with a heart rate strap, the app sends the heart rate along that run as at most 64 whole numbers (beats per minute, one for each point of the elevation profile), plus the heart rate zone (1 to 5) your phone worked out for each of them from your own zone settings, so the coach can draw a "Heart vs hill" picture in your own Telegram chat. Nothing else about your heart is sent: no resting rate, no maximum, no times and no dates. Our server keeps the series for 30 days after its run, then deletes it, and it is in the data the server returns about you. It is left out of the server's nightly backup, so turning the switch off really removes everything. Turning "Heart rate on cards" off deletes every series we hold at once, as soon as your phone can reach our server, or the next time the app connects if you were offline, and the phone stops sending it straight away. The same picture is only drawn for you: if you forward it from Telegram, anyone you send it to can see what it shows.

Quests

The app can offer you one quest a month: a target made from your own history, such as running a trail more times in 30 days than you ever have, or climbing more in 30 days than you ever have. Your phone works out the quest and your progress. It never compares you with anyone else. When the app offers one, it sends the companion up to three offers (each a kind, a target and your own best, as whole numbers), and, once you take one, the quest you took with how far along you are and the date it ends, and, when it ends, how (finished, dropped or lapsed), or that you chose Not this month. The trail's name goes only if you turned on the place option, otherwise the message says "your trail".

With that, the companion sends your own Telegram chat one message a month offering the quest, with Take it, Another and Not this month buttons. It counts inside the same weekly limit as suggestions. If you take a quest, the run that finishes it gets one extra line on its card, and when 7 days are left and you are well short, the next evening suggestion may carry one line about it, once. The companion sends no nudge while it is keeping things light after a check-in answer. If you take or skip a quest in Telegram, the app can ask the server for it when it asks for your next session, so both agree.

The server keeps these as a small table (the kind, target, your best, progress, state, end date, the month it was offered and when you took it, and the trail name only with the place option). It deletes a quest 90 days after it ends, or 90 days after the month it was offered in if you never took it, and turning the place option off deletes the names at once. The server also remembers which of your already uploaded runs finished a quest, so that run's card can carry the line. The quests are in the data the server returns about you, and Delete everything removes them. Backups can keep them for up to 14 days. They never go to OpenRouter, the model provider or the card renderer: every quest line is a fixed template filled in on our server.

Paused weeks. The app's weekly streak can pause a week when you are sore or ill. If you answered a check-in with sore or ill, the server tells the app which weeks (as Monday dates, for the last 8 weeks that its 14-day check-in answers still cover) it counted as such, so the streak can pause. It does not say which of the two it was, or why. You can also pause a week yourself in the app, and that stays on your phone.

The route outline

With "Show route shape on cards" on, the app sends an outline of each run's route so the companion can draw it on the picture or video of that run. On your phone, before anything is sent: a hidden circle around the places you start or finish is cut off each end of the route first, and then a random 150 to 300 metres more (a new length each time, for each end), so the outline does not show exactly where you started or finished. We clip a hidden zone around places you start or finish. The zone is kept only on your phone: it is never sent to us, and it stays the same from run to run, so many runs from the same door do not add up to the door; the rest is thinned to at most 120 points; it is turned so its longest direction runs left to right; it is moved to the corner of a square and scaled so its longer side is exactly 255 steps; and each point is rounded to a whole number from 0 to 255. What is sent is that list of whole numbers. It has no coordinates (no latitude or longitude) and no compass direction. Our server refuses anything that is not in that form.

What this does not do. It is still the real shape of the middle of your route. Our server also holds the suburb name and the distance of the run, so it can work out roughly how big the outline is and the area it is in. A distinctive outline could therefore still be recognised, or matched to streets, by someone who knows the route or who gets hold of our data. The cut ends protect exactly where you start and finish, not the route in between. If that matters to you, turn the switch off: your cards then show your splits and zone bar instead, and the app sends no outline. Turning it off deletes the outlines the server already holds (as soon as your phone can reach it; backups can keep them for up to 14 days), and the app stops sending them. Outlines already drawn into pictures in your Telegram chat stay there until you delete them. Your per-kilometre paces and rep times are separate from the outline and stay with the rest of the run summary.

Turning on "Weather in suggestions" first shows a sheet with what is sent, to whom and why. With the switch off, no area leaves your phone for the companion. Runs sent after you turn it on carry the area; runs sent before do not. The weather part is described under "Where it goes" and "How long we keep it" below.

The companion also keeps your answers to its questions. It asks at most one question a day, counted on your own calendar day, and only when your answer would change what it suggests next. A weekly set of background questions (what you are training for, the month of your race, when you usually run, and whether you have a niggle: none, calf, knee, hip, foot or other) comes under the same limit. You answer with buttons. It records whether you take or skip a suggestion. A Hard tap on a run also makes that evening's suggestion gentler.

Check-ins

The companion can check in with you, with buttons and nothing typed. Each answer edits the same message in your chat.

  • The evening before a planned session: how the body feels (Fresh, Normal, Flat, Sore somewhere, Not tomorrow).
  • The morning of a planned session, about 15 minutes before the time you usually start and never inside your quiet hours: Run, Move (a walk, strength or mobility instead) or Skip. Skip asks why (Tired, Sore, Ill, Busy, Weather); Busy offers 20 easy minutes today. The planned session moves to a later day. On a hot day the card adds one line with the extra seconds per km the heat costs on an easy pace; it is worked out from the forecast for your approximate area, only if you have turned on Weather in suggestions.
  • After an easy run that came out slower and harder than usual, and only then: what was behind it (Short sleep, Stressed, Heat, Nothing).
  • On Sunday: how many runs you want next week (3, 4, 5 or Away).

The answers are fixed choices, not text you type. Soreness and illness are health information. The companion uses them for one thing, to go lighter: for 3 days after a skip for tiredness or soreness, and for a week if you say you are ill (Busy and Weather only move the session). When you say sore or ill it also tells you to get it looked at or to see a doctor if it hangs around. It gives no medical advice and makes no diagnosis.

These answers stay on our server and in your chat. For the companion's notes they are never sent to OpenRouter or the model provider, to Open-Meteo or to the card renderer: every line that uses one is a fixed template filled in on our server, and a run note can carry one such line after the writing service has finished. If you turn on the coach chat, the coach reads your check-in answers and run notes from the last 14 days to reply, so they go to an AI provider (OpenRouter and Anthropic, in the US) with zero data retention, as in "Coach chat" below. The picture of a moved session shows only that it moved and to which day, never why. A pinned card for the week shows your planned and finished distances per day. We keep each answer for 14 days and then delete it. They are left out of the nightly backup, so a backup never holds them. They are in the data the server returns about you, and the app lists them under What the companion knows. Delete everything removes them at once.

The server also keeps a small record of which check-in messages it sent, by day, so it never sends one twice and keeps to one question a day. That record holds no answer and goes after 14 days too, as do the stored texts of the check-in messages themselves. The app can ask the server for your next planned session and the latest note the companion sent; the reply says whether the session was moved, a quest you took in Telegram, and the Monday dates of recent weeks you said you were sore or ill (see "Quests"), and nothing else about your answers.

Your agreement. When you tap Agree on the companion card in the app, the app tells our server which version of the agreement you accepted (a single number, sent with your device token). The companion sends no check-in until that number is the current one, so if you have not agreed to the latest version, or the app has not said so, you get no check-ins; run cards and your other companion messages carry on as before. The number is deleted with everything else when you delete your data.

Notes you add. On Telegram, each run note in your chat has an Add a note button. Tap it and the next message you type (up to 500 characters, within 10 minutes) is saved as a note on that run. This is the only typed text the companion keeps. It is used in two plain ways only: the Sunday recap may quote your note back to you, and a few words (niggle, sore, injury, tired, sick) make the next suggestions gentler for a week. A note is never sent to OpenRouter or any AI provider, and never leaves our server except in your own chat. Anything else you type to the companion, except feedback you send with Tell Ricki (below), gets a fixed reply and is not kept.

Tell Ricki. Tap Tell Ricki on the Sunday card, or send /feedback, and the next message you type (up to 1000 characters, within 30 minutes) is saved as feedback. Ricki reads it. It is never sent to OpenRouter or any AI provider, and the reply is a fixed line. We keep it for 30 days and then delete it, it is left out of the nightly backup, and it is deleted at once when you delete your data.

Counting taps and activity. To judge whether the coach is useful, the server counts what you do with it, once you have agreed to the companion terms. Each time you tap a button, add a note, send feedback, send a command such as STOP, send any other message, or open the Mini App, it records the kind and the time, never the words. Those records are kept for 120 days, are deleted when you delete your data, and are left out of the nightly backup. It also counts the cards it sent and the runs and notes you add. Each runner is a tester number such as T3, not a name, chat id or message text, so the counts are per person but pseudonymous. From them we work out, per tester and per week, whether you are still using it in week 4, how many replies you send, and what the writing service cost for your cards.

Two questions are answered with fixed buttons. The day after the Sunday recap, the coach asks whether last week's coaching got you right (Spot on, Mostly or Not really). Once, about three to four weeks after you join, it asks how you would feel if the coach went away. Each counts as that day's one question, and neither is sent to an AI provider. We keep these answers until the test ends, and they are deleted when you delete your data. When anyone sends DELETE or STOP, the server adds one to a count for that week, with no id, so the totals are right after the person is gone. We use all of this only to improve the companion, and never for ads.

During the test Ricki can also send a few fixed, pre-written messages through the coach, such as a nudge if you have not run yet or an invite to a chat. Which one was sent to you and when is logged against your tester number (not the words), kept until the test ends, and deleted when you delete your data. The words themselves stay with the other messages the coach has sent you, for 30 days, like any card.

Coach chat

The coach chat is optional and off until you agree to it in the app (Settings, Companion, the companion agreement, version 3). It is for questions about your training, in your own Telegram chat. The coach can answer your questions about your training in Telegram, and write short spoken briefings before your runs. To reply, your run summaries, check-in answers and what you type go to an AI provider (OpenRouter and Anthropic, in the US) with zero data retention. Your GPS tracks and heart rate recordings stay on your phone. Chats are kept for 30 days. They are then deleted, and are left out of the nightly backup. You can turn the coach off any time in Settings, Companion, and that deletes your chats and any coach memory. It happens at once. We record when you agreed. The provider may not keep or train on what it receives. The coach gives running guidance, not medical advice. If you tell it about chest pain, fainting or a serious injury it stops giving training advice and tells you to get help.

Coach memory

The coach can remember you between chats, but only after you agree to it in the app (the companion agreement, version 4). The one exception is the person who runs the test, whose memory is switched on by hand until he agrees in the app too. Memory is off for everyone else, and with it off the coach still talks to you; it just starts from zero each time. With it on, the server keeps short notes: facts you tell the coach to keep (your goals, schedule, what you like, key runs; up to 20 of up to 160 characters each) and one short summary of your earlier chats (up to 700 characters, rewritten every ten messages you send). The app also records whether the training numbers switch is on (it starts on). Sending your training numbers (estimated race times, best efforts, weekly load and your plan) is not built yet: when it is, the coach will keep them only while that switch is on, and this page will say so. Health notes (niggles, injuries and illness) are separate: the coach keeps them only if you tick "Keep health notes too", which starts unticked. With it unticked the coach will not save anything about injury, pain or illness, and it leaves them out of the chat summary. Your GPS tracks and heart rate recordings are not part of coach memory and stay on your phone.

The notes are stored on our server in Germany. To write the summary, the notes and your recent messages go to OpenRouter and the model provider (outside the EU, in the US), under a zero-data-retention setting: the provider may not keep or train on them. Nothing in your notes is written to our server logs. You can see every note by sending /memory in Telegram, and delete any of them. Notes are kept until you delete them or delete your data; they are not purged at 30 days like chat messages. The live database is not yet encrypted at rest; backups are encrypted.

Deleting it. Delete everything (in the app), DELETE (in the chat) or the web delete page removes all coach memory from the live server at once. In the app, Settings, Companion, Coach memory, Turn off memory deletes all of it at once and keeps your check-ins and the coach. Unticking "Keep health notes too" deletes the health notes and the chat summary at once. Coach memory is included in the nightly backup, so it is gone from the live server immediately and from every backup within 14 days; if we ever restore a backup, we delete again what you had already deleted before the coach is switched back on.

Your phone number or Telegram chat

The companion has no account. It knows you by your chat. To connect, the app opens Telegram, you tap Start, and the app asks you to type back a 6-digit code the chat sends you. The server then holds your Telegram chat id and chat name. For WhatsApp, once it is available, the server will hold your phone number, which WhatsApp gives us when you send your first message. You do not type your phone number into the app. The app keeps a connection key in your phone's secure storage, and the server keeps only a scrambled copy of it.

When the test is full. While the companion is in testing it connects a limited number of runners (25). If you ask after that, the chat gets a short reply saying so, and we keep nothing about you: not your Telegram chat or name. The only record is a count of how many people asked that day, with nothing to say who. (A hashed copy of the message's id is kept for 30 days to stop the same message being handled twice, like every other message.)

Flooding and cost limits. The server counts how many messages a chat sends in a minute and in a day, in memory only, to slow down a flood. If a connected runner is slowed down three times in 24 hours, they are paused automatically (the server keeps one row per slow-down for 2 days to count them), and Ricki can pause or un-pause a runner by hand. A paused runner gets no messages, but sending DELETE still erases everything. The server also records the cost of each call to the writing service against the runner it was for, so the monthly spend can be capped and checked per runner. That record holds a cost, a time and a model name, no text. When you delete your data it stays only as an anonymous cost line, with nothing linking it to you.

Where it goes

  • Our server, hosted by Hetzner in Germany. It stores your run summaries, your answers, the notes it sent you, and your chat details, in a database that only the companion service can read. That includes each run's per-kilometre paces and rep times and, unless you turned it off, its route outline. With "Weather in suggestions" on, it also holds one rounded area, from your latest run that had one.
  • Open-Meteo, a weather service, only if you turn on "Weather in suggestions". Our server sends it that rounded area (about 11 km) and your time zone, to ask for the forecast there. No name, account or device ID goes with it. Open-Meteo sees our server's IP address, not yours. See section 3 for the weather request the app itself makes.
  • OpenRouter, and the AI provider it routes to, which write the words of each note. They receive only the facts of the run as short sentences (for a trail run, whether the companion read it as easy, tempo or long from its distance, time and heart rate zones; and, when it compares two runs, your True Pace for both), and your answers to the background questions as fixed options. They never receive your check-in answers, phone number, Telegram chat, name, any note you type, or the route outline, per-kilometre paces or rep times, which are used only to draw the picture. We ask for providers that do not keep or train on what they receive (zero retention), and the request is refused if none is available. If the writing service fails, the note is written from a template on our server instead.
  • Telegram, which delivers the notes and sees them like any message in a chat. Telegram's own privacy policy applies to the chat.
  • Meta (WhatsApp), only once WhatsApp is switched on and you choose it. Meta delivers the messages and keeps WhatsApp messages for up to 30 days. Meta's own privacy policy applies to the chat.

We do not sell this data, use it for advertising, or share it with anyone else.

How long we keep it

  • Run summaries, answers and your chat details are kept until you delete them, or until six months pass with no run, when your companion data is deleted automatically.
  • The record of notes we sent is kept for 30 days.
  • Notes you add are kept as long as the run they belong to, so until you delete your data or six months pass with no run. They are included in the data the server returns about you, but the app does not show them. Delete everything removes them. A Sunday recap that quotes a note carries that quote only in your chat: our server does not keep a copy of it, so replacing or deleting the note stops it being quoted.
  • Per-kilometre paces and rep times are kept with the run they belong to, so until you delete your data or six months pass with no run. The route outline, and the trail times, elevation profile and heart rate series, are kept for 30 days after their run, then deleted. All of them are in the data the server returns about you (the app does not show them). Turning "Show route shape on cards" off deletes the outlines at once, as soon as your phone can reach our server, or the next time the app connects if you were offline.
  • The rounded weather area: only the latest one is kept. It is deleted 30 days after the run it came from, when you turn "Weather in suggestions" off (as soon as your phone can reach our server, or the next time the app connects if you were offline), when you re-connect the companion, and when you delete your data. Backups can keep it for up to 14 days.
  • A niggle answer expires after 8 weeks.
  • Quests (the kind, target, your best, progress, state, end date and, with the place option, the trail name) are kept until 90 days after the quest ends, or 90 days after the month it was offered in if you never took it. They are in the data the server returns about you. The names go at once when you turn the place option off.
  • Check-in answers (how the body feels, why you skipped, what was behind a slow easy run, how many runs next week) are kept for 14 days, then deleted. The record of which check-ins were sent goes after 14 days too. Neither is in the nightly backup.
  • An unfinished connection (you never entered the code) is deleted after an hour.
  • The cost of each call to the writing service is kept for 400 days, linked to you until you delete your data and anonymous after that. The slow-down record that pauses a flood is kept for 2 days. The count of people turned away when the test was full is kept for 90 days.
  • Backups: the server database is backed up each night, encrypted, and each backup is deleted after 14 days. Data you delete is gone from the live server at once and from the backups within 14 days.
  • Server logs hide phone numbers, chat ids and keys.

Notes already in your Telegram or WhatsApp chat stay there until you delete them yourself.

How to delete it

  • In the app: Settings, Companion, Delete everything. Settings, Companion also shows what the companion holds about you. Turn off does the same as Delete everything: it deletes everything the server holds about you and disconnects the app. To pause the notes without deleting anything, send STOP in the chat instead.
  • In the chat: send DELETE to the companion. It erases everything held about you and confirms. STOP pauses the messages, and RESUME turns them back on.
  • Without the app: use the steps at runsupreme.taiaroa.xyz/delete, which work even if the app is uninstalled.

Deleting removes every row we hold about you from the live database. Meta may keep delivery records for up to 30 days, and backups age out within 14 days, as above. In the app, Turn off and Delete everything both delete your data; only STOP in the chat pauses the notes and keeps it. Turning "Weather in suggestions" off deletes just the stored weather area. Turning "Show route shape on cards" off deletes just the stored route outlines.

2c. Run companion: images and videos

This applies only if you turned the optional Run companion on and it has been set to send pictures. Its notes can then arrive in your Telegram chat as an image or a short video instead of plain text. The picture shows more than the text does. Most of it comes from the run summaries you already sent the companion: the date and start time, the type of run, distance, duration and pace, any climb, your time in each heart rate zone, the name of the suburb or town of the run (never the street), any personal best, for an interval session its work pace (hills and heat taken out), for a quality session planned for today the pace the app targets for it (shown as a TARGET plate on the morning picture), and, on the weekly recap, how your scores moved and the week's average pace and quickest run (both worked out from the paces and distances you already sent), plus the coach's one-line note for that run. In your chat, a run picture's caption is that one line of judgement under a short title; the figures are on the picture itself. Unless you turned off "Show route shape on cards", a run picture or video can also draw an outline of your route with its ends cut off (section 2b): no coordinates and no street, but a distinctive route could still be recognised by someone who knows it. It never contains a map, your name or a phone number, and no heart rate readings unless you turned on "Heart rate on cards" (the Heart vs hill picture below). The per-kilometre paces or rep times can be drawn as bars.

Five more kinds of picture show a little beyond the run summary itself:

  • Tomorrow's suggestion. The date, the session (for example "Tempo, 8 km") and a one-line reason. When the reason is the weather (heat, rain, or good conditions) it carries the forecast: the temperature, the hour and the chance of rain. That forecast comes from Open-Meteo (section 3), and only if you turned on "Weather in suggestions".
  • The monthly poster. On the 1st, a calendar of the month just ended: for each run its day, type of run, distance and time, plus the month's totals, including its average pace (total time over total distance). No route and no place names.
  • Trail board and trail record. Your own runs of one trail, ranked by time, the trail's elevation profile shaded by steepness, and where you gained or lost time against your best (section 2b, "Trail pictures"). Your own runs only, no dates, no map.
  • Heart vs hill. Only if you turned on "Heart rate on cards": your heart rate along a trail run, as at most 64 bpm values coloured by your own zones, drawn over the trail's elevation profile (section 2b, "Heart rate on trail pictures"). Your own runs only, no dates, no map.
  • The monthly quest. One message offering a quest made from your own history (section 2b, "Quests"), and, if you finish it, a line on that run's card. A fixed template: no model writes it.
  • Milestones and race countdowns. The milestone number (for example your 100th run, or 1,000 km) and the weeks to your race, with the distance you told the coach you are training for.

For the suggestion, milestone and countdown pictures the companion keeps the few facts the picture was drawn from, with the message, for 30 days. They are part of what "What the companion knows" and the export show (as picture_facts, including messages built but not yet sent) and are removed when you delete your data. The monthly poster keeps nothing extra: it is drawn again from the runs you already sent.

The picture is drawn on the companion's server by a separate process that has no internet access, no database and none of your account details; it receives only those figures and the route outline, returns the image, and keeps nothing. The finished image is then sent to your Telegram chat, so Telegram stores it under its own policy, and it stays in your chat until you delete it there. Our server does not keep the image. If you do not want pictures, turn the companion off (which deletes your data) or send STOP in the chat to pause it.

3. Weather from Open-Meteo

To adjust for heat and humidity, Run Supreme can fetch the weather for each run from Open-Meteo. The request contains your run's location rounded to about 10 km, and the run's date and time. It contains no identifier: no account, no device ID, no name. Open-Meteo's servers see the IP address the request comes from, as any web request does. Weather data is provided by Open-Meteo.com under the CC BY 4.0 licence.

You can turn this off in Settings, under Recording, with the "Weather for each run" switch. With it off, the app itself sends nothing to Open-Meteo. The Run companion has its own separate switch, "Weather in suggestions", off until you turn it on, described in section 2b.

Place names

Each run shows where it started, for example "Lakeside Dr, Albert Park". Run Supreme asks Android's built-in geocoder (android.location.Geocoder) to turn the start point of the run into a street name (never a house number) and a short area name. Run Supreme makes no network request for this itself. The Android geocoder is part of your phone's system and may use Google Play services, which can send the coordinate to Google to get the name back, under the Google Privacy Policy. Only the start point is looked up, never the route, your pace or your heart rate. The names are stored on your phone with the run; if the lookup fails, the run simply has no place name. A run that starts within about 300 m of an earlier run reuses that run's area name, and within about 60 m its street name, without a new lookup. A run saved before street names existed is looked up once, when you open it.

You can rename a run yourself from its detail screen; the title is stored only on your phone.

5. What we do not do

  • No accounts, sign-in or profiles. The optional Run companion identifies you only by your Telegram chat (or, later, your WhatsApp number).
  • No advertising, no ad SDKs.
  • No analytics or tracking of our own. The only analytics-type signal is the Maps SDK identifier described in section 2, which belongs to Google's SDK, not to us.
  • We do not sell or share your data. Unless you turn on the Run companion, our servers hold none of it. If you do, sections 2b and 2c say what we hold and for how long.
  • Run Supreme is not directed at children and has no features aimed at them.
